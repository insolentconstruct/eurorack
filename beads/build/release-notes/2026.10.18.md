**Code name: Mist.** **Beta 1 is a call for testers.** New options default to stock behaviour, so the module behaves like Mutable Instruments' firmware until you change them; settings saved by the alphas are kept. All hardware testing so far used one module. Please try it on yours and [report what you find](#reporting-problems), good or bad.

## New features

Listed by the number of requests on the [roadmap](https://github.com/insolentconstruct/eurorack/issues/32).

Most settings live on the **options page**: hold the CV-assign button [M] for three seconds, then release. Tap [M] to change page, and turn a mix knob to change its setting; it saves by itself about a second after you stop. Close by holding [M] again or pressing another button. A printable guide, a phone guide and a text guide come in the zip.

- **Clouds-style reverb** (page 1, REVERB; 11 requests, #2 and #22): the Clouds reverb's timings and modulation, plus diffusion on the grain output.
- **Buffer trim** (4 requests, #31): hold the quality button [A], then press FREEZE to start a fresh recording window; the next freeze keeps only what was recorded since. Saved buffers keep the trimmed length. This is Mutable Instruments' own unreleased gesture.
- **Stereo width** (page 1, DRY/WET; 4 requests, #5): spreads grains randomly across the stereo field. It can be added as a fourth [M] destination for CV control.
- **Delay mode** (page 2, DRY/WET; 4 requests, #9 and #26): Auto (Stock), Never (SIZE fully clockwise stays granular) or Wide detent (a neutral zone in the middle of the delay filter, without the resonant boost).
- **Pitch scales** (page 1, FEEDBACK; 4 requests, #6 and #8): the knob selects Off, Chromatic or Scale; with Scale selected, tap SEED to step through eight scales from Marbles. Each grain's pitch, including randomness, snaps to the scale when the grain starts.
- **Wavetable synth on demand** (page 3, FEEDBACK; 3 requests, #17): Automatic (Stock) starts the wavetable synth 10 seconds after both inputs are unplugged, as before. Manual never starts it by itself: with both inputs unplugged and FREEZE off, hold [A] for about a second and a half to start or stop it.
- **Monophonic grains** (2 requests, #31): hold [A], then press SEED. This is Mutable Instruments' own unreleased code. It works best with the square and decay envelopes.
- **Output limiter** (page 3, DRY/WET): Stock, Fast release, or Off.

### The options page changed since the alphas

Options now sit on three pages. Changes save by themselves, the page stays open until you close it, and it reopens on the page you left until power-off.

| Page (pulsing LED) | FEEDBACK | DRY/WET | REVERB |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (REVERB) | Pitch scale | Stereo width | Reverb character |
| 2 (FEEDBACK) | Not used yet | Delay mode | Not used yet |
| 3 (DRY/WET) | Wavetable synth | Limiter | Not used yet |

The limiter and self-play settings you saved on alpha 1 or 2 carry over unchanged. Both are now on page 3, and self-play is now called Wavetable synth, with On request renamed Manual.

## Bug fixes

Compared with the source Mutable Instruments released:

- **Flash wear from a modulated FREEZE.** Stock firmware starts a new save of the frozen buffer each time FREEZE stays on for 10 seconds, so an LFO or sequence on FREEZE can wear out the flash: a 20-second LFO is up to about 4,000 erases a day, against a rating of 10,000. Drizzlet waits longer after each save that gets cut short, up to about five minutes, so a modulated FREEZE now writes once instead of every cycle. A completed save, or a minute with FREEZE off, resets the wait.
- **Frozen-buffer saves:** each save carries a checksum and is range-checked on load. An interrupted or failed save is never restored. Changing a setting during a save no longer breaks the save. Stock and Drizzlet still load each other's saves.
- **Frozen delay:** negative FM no longer reads outside the buffer, and very fast SEED edges no longer create a zero-length slice that silenced the play heads.
- **Delay clock:** no tempo glitch on the first clock after about 12 hours without one.
- **Undefined memory reads** fixed in two places, with no audible change.

## Faster, same sound

The app is now built with GCC 9 instead of GCC 4.9.3, which is about 2% faster. Three small code changes, written in Mutable Instruments' style, then cut the heaviest stock grain case from 97,255 to 90,333 cycles per block, freeing about 6% of the audio budget (measured on a development board). The sound is bit-identical to the released source in every emulated comparison. [OPTIMIZATIONS.md](https://github.com/insolentconstruct/eurorack/blob/{TAG}/beads/build/OPTIMIZATIONS.md) has the details.

On the module, a full audio block uses 60% to 90.5% of the budget, depending on the settings, with no audio underruns.

## Coming from the 2021 or 2024 firmware

Drizzlet also includes everything Mutable Instruments changed after those releases. That includes the settings recovery for modules stuck on boot, smoother knobs and delay controls, and the grain-scheduling change after which our module no longer crackled at high DENSITY. [CHANGELOG.md](https://github.com/insolentconstruct/eurorack/blob/{TAG}/beads/build/CHANGELOG.md) lists every difference.

## How it was tested

- **Host tests:** 164 tests in 13 programs run on every push. They cover 110 of the 117 behaviours in the manual and include 538 injected power-loss and flash-error points. 152 deliberate faults check that the tests catch them.
- **Builds:** reproducible to pinned hashes.
- **Emulator:** identical audio to the previous builds wherever nothing was meant to change.
- **On a module:** about 13 hours of automated runs, driven by a Bela, an audio interface, an oscilloscope and a debug probe. Scenarios shared with the released source were compared against it, and no difference traced back to the firmware. All nine pitch scales landed on their scale notes.
- **Firmware installs:** the test module has been flashed 48 times, with Mutable Instruments' builds and Drizzlet, and never failed to boot.

Details are in [TESTING.md](https://github.com/insolentconstruct/eurorack/blob/{TAG}/beads/build/TESTING.md).

## Code style

New code follows the existing Mutable Instruments Beads style; see [STYLE.md](https://github.com/insolentconstruct/eurorack/blob/{TAG}/beads/build/STYLE.md). Features reuse Mutable Instruments' own code where it exists: monophonic grains, buffer trim, the Clouds reverb and the Marbles scales.

## Downgrading

- Turn monophonic grains off first.
- Stock firmware loads trimmed saves at full length.
- Saving settings on stock firmware clears the Drizzlet options.

## Known limits

- Tested on one module only.
- As in Mutable Instruments' firmware, about once every 4,000 settings saves the settings memory is erased and rewritten. Cutting the power at that moment can reset calibration, settings or both to defaults. After changing settings, wait a second before powering off.
- The options page's LED colours have not been checked by eye on a module.
- Where the Beads manual and the firmware disagree, see [MANUAL_ERRATA.md](https://github.com/insolentconstruct/eurorack/blob/{TAG}/beads/build/MANUAL_ERRATA.md).
